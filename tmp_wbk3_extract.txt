Every morning, students arrive at school with their backpacks. The bell rings at 7:30 a.m., and
they go to their classrooms. In one classroom, the teacher is already there, smiling and
preparing the lesson. Books, notebooks, and pencils are on each desk. On the wall, there is a
large clock and a flag. The students sit quietly, waiting to begin. Today, the teacher is reading a
short story to the class. One student is writing the date on the board. Everyone is ready to learn.
Sometimes, the students arrive early and talk to each other before cl ass begins. They share their
homework, laugh about jokes, and help each other prepare. The classroom is bright and
colorful, with posters on the walls and a bookshelf in the corner.
During the lesson, the teacher explains the topic with examples. She asks questions and
encourages everyone to participat e. The students listen, write in their notebooks, and answer
when called. Learning becomes more interesting when everyone works together.
After the lesson, the students wo rk in small groups to complete an activity. They discuss the
topic and share their ideas. Some students draw pictures to explain their answers, while others
write short sentences. The teacher walks around the classroom, listening and giving support.
Everyone is engaged and learning from each other.

Present Simple – Affirmative, Negative, and Interrogative
We use the present simple to talk about routines, habits, and facts.
Affirmative:
The students read stories.
Mrs. Clark drinks coffee.
Negative:
The students do not watch TV in class.
She does not teach on Saturday.
Interrogative:
Do the students read every day?
Does Mrs. Clark give homework?
Note:
Use do/does in questions.
Add s to verbs with he/she/it.

In Mrs. Clark's bustling classroom, the day begins with a warm greeting.
Mrs. Clark: Hello, students! How are you today?
Students: Hello, Mrs. Clark. We are fine.
The classroom is filled with diverse personalities, and the teacher, Mrs. Clark, decides to spark
a conversation about studying.
Mrs. Clark: Great to hear! Let's talk about studying. Do students like to study?
Students: Some like it; some don't.
Mrs. Clark acknowledges the variety of preferences among her students. The dialogue unfolds
as they discuss the different attitudes towards learning.
Mrs. Clark: Yes, many students enjoy learning new things. But some don’t.
Students: Yes, many students like to study.
Encouraged by their responses, Mrs. Clark navigates the conversation toward expressing
contrasting opinions.
Mrs. Clark: Now, what if we want to say the opposite? Do all students like to study?
Students: No, not all students like to study.
