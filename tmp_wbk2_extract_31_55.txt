For example, water in the glass is uncountable because we can’t count the water itself, but we use words like some to describe it: "There is some water in the glass." Similarly, hair is uncountable; even though Pat, Bob, and Billy all have hair, we don’t count each individual strand of hair. Instead, we say, "Pat has brown hair," or "Billy has blond hair," using uncountable nouns to describe the general concept of hair.
