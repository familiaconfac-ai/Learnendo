Title: **Commonly Confused Verbs**

Body:

## Say or Tell?

Use **say** to focus on the words or message. Use **tell** when you name the person who receives the information.

### Structures

- **say + something**: She said the answer. / She said hello.
- **say + something + to + someone**: She said hello to me. / What did you say to John?
- **tell + someone + something**: She told me the answer. / Can you tell me the truth?
- Questions: What did she say? / Who told you that?

### Contrast

> Example: She said the answer. / She told me the answer.

With **say**, put **to** before the person: *She said the answer to me.* With **tell**, put the person directly after the verb: *She told me the answer.*

## Hear or Listen?

Use **hear** when a sound reaches your ears. Use **listen** when you pay attention to a sound on purpose.

### Structures

- **hear + a sound / someone**: I can hear music. / I heard a noise. / Can you hear me?
- **listen + to + a sound/person**: Listen to the teacher. / I am listening to music. / Are you listening to me?

### Contrast

> Example: I can hear music. / I am listening to music.

You may hear a sound without trying. When you listen, you choose to pay attention. Use **listen to** before a person or thing.

### Questions

Can you hear the school bell? / What are you listening to? / Are you listening to the teacher?

## Borrow or Lend?

**Borrow** describes receiving something temporarily. **Lend** describes giving something temporarily.

### Structures

- **borrow + something + from + someone**: I borrowed a pencil from John. / Can I borrow your pencil?
- **lend + someone + something**: John lent me a pencil. / Can you lend me your pencil?
- **lend + something + to + someone**: John lent a pencil to me.

### Contrast

> Example: I borrowed a pencil from John. / John lent me a pencil. / John lent a pencil to me.

The same exchange can be described from either person's point of view: the person receiving **borrows**; the person giving **lends**.

### Questions

Can I borrow your book? / Who did you borrow the book from? / Can you lend me a pen? / Who lent the book to you?

## Common Mistakes

- Incorrect: She said me the truth. Correct: She told me the truth. Also correct: She said the truth to me.
- Incorrect: Listen me, please. Correct: Listen to me, please.
- Incorrect: I listened a noise. Correct: I heard a noise.
- Incorrect: Can you borrow me your pencil? Correct: Can you lend me your pencil?
- Incorrect when asking to receive: Can I lend your pencil? Correct: Can I borrow your pencil?
- Incorrect: I borrowed a book of my friend. Correct: I borrowed a book from my friend.

## Everyday School Examples

The teacher said the answer to the class. / The teacher told us the answer.

I heard the bell, then I listened to the teacher's instructions.

I borrowed a book from Anna. / Anna lent me a book. / Anna lent a book to me.
